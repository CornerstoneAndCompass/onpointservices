What we trim

Any hedge, any height, any species.

A hedge done well is a thing of beauty. A hedge done badly is a permanent eyesore that takes years to recover. The difference is mostly experience: knowing how hard to cut, when to cut, and reading the shape that already wants to be there.

  • Boxwood, buxus and formal low hedges
  • Griselinia, totara, taupata coastal hedges
  • Macrocarpa, leyland and tall shelter hedges
  • Photinia, pittosporum, pencil pine
  • Topiary, cones, cubes, balls, spirals
  • Renovation cuts on neglected hedges

How we do tall hedges safely.

Anything over 3 m needs proper access. We turn up with the right gear instead of stretching off a stepladder.

Hedge FAQs.

How often should hedges be trimmed?

Most hedges want two trims a year, late spring and late summer, to hold a tight shape. Faster growers like privet or photinia can want a third trim. Slow growers like boxwood are happy on one or two.

Can you save a hedge that has been left for years?

Usually yes. Most species can take a hard renovation cut and bounce back inside two seasons, but it depends on the species and condition. We will tell you honestly at site visit if a hedge is worth saving or worth replacing.

Do you take the clippings away?

Yes, every visit, included in the price. No piles left for you to deal with.

What about my neighbour's side?

If we can access it safely we will trim both sides for a tidier finish, with neighbour permission. We always ask first.
